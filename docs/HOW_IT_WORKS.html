The idea in one breath

Read the web about a prospect, match it to Zoho, hand the rep a briefing.

Before a sales call, a good rep spends the night before searching the web about the company they're meeting — the recent news, the funding, who's who, what they might need. SalesIntel AI does that reading, organizing, and first-draft writing automatically, and produces a one-page briefing where every claim links back to where it came from.

The plain-English version: point it at a company. It quietly reads ~10–15 public web pages about them, keeps only the sales-relevant facts, checks how trustworthy each source is, matches the company's likely needs to Zoho's product line, and writes a tailored, cited briefing — ready by the time the rep sits down.

The data journey

How a company name becomes a briefing.

Here's the path your data actually takes. The important thing to notice: almost all of it is ordinary, reliable software — no AI involved. The AI is used once, near the end, to write.

1

Start with a company plain software

Either a meeting arrives from your Google Calendar (synced automatically every 30 seconds) or you type one in. The company name kicks everything off.

2

Read the open web no AI

Keyless search plus Wikipedia and public news/filings surface the most relevant pages about the company — no paid data providers.

3

Extract the real text no AI

Each page is stripped down to its actual article — headlines, navigation, and ads discarded — so only the substance remains.

4

Keep only what matters no AI

A keyword guide (built from the client's research playbook) filters the text down to sales-relevant passages — funding, leadership changes, pain signals — and tags each by topic.

5

Rate each source no AI

A simple rulebook scores trustworthiness: an official site or regulatory filing ranks high; a random forum post ranks low. That score travels with every fact.

6

Bring in the Zoho catalog no AI

The prospect's likely needs are matched against the pre-built catalog of Zoho products, so the briefing can point to specific, relevant offerings.

7

Write the briefing AI · one step

Only now does the AI step in. Gemini reads the prepared, trust-scored, cited notes and writes the nine-section brief — shaped to the reader's role and the meeting's agenda.

8

Deliver it AI for chat

The brief appears on screen, downloads as a PDF, and is emailed to the rep. Follow-up questions to Scout are answered by AI, grounded in the same sources.

Why this order matters: steps 1–6 do the heavy lifting with plain software — free, fast, and fully traceable, because every fact keeps a link to its source. The AI is handed a tidy, cited packet and asked only to write it up. That's what keeps the briefing grounded instead of guessy.

The output

A one-page briefing — cited, and shaped to your role.

For a Sales Representative, the briefing is nine sections. Every claim carries a small citation marker back to its source, a confidence level, and a badge showing how trustworthy that source is.
